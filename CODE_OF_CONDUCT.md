# Bourbon Community Code of Conduct 🥃

Bourbon is built by developers, contributors, testers, users, and members of the wider macOS and Wine communities.

We want Bourbon's spaces to be useful, welcoming, and comfortable for people who are here to build, test, troubleshoot, learn, or help others.

This Code of Conduct applies to participation in the Bourbon project and its official community spaces.

## Our Commitment

Everyone participating in Bourbon should be treated with respect regardless of:

- Age
- Experience or technical skill level
- Disability
- Ethnicity or nationality
- Gender identity or expression
- Personal appearance
- Race
- Religion
- Sex or sexual orientation
- Socio-economic background
- Operating system, hardware, or development environment
- Whether they are a developer, tester, contributor, or regular user

You do not need to be an expert in Swift, Wine, macOS, or Windows compatibility to participate in the community.

Questions asked in good faith should be treated as such.

## What We Expect

Good participation in the Bourbon community includes:

- Treating other people with respect.
- Giving technical feedback without attacking the person who wrote the code.
- Being patient with users who are troubleshooting unfamiliar software.
- Providing useful reproduction steps, logs, screenshots, or other information when reporting bugs.
- Accepting constructive review of your own work.
- Disagreeing with ideas without turning disagreements into personal arguments.
- Acknowledging mistakes and correcting them when appropriate.
- Respecting the time of maintainers, contributors, and volunteer testers.
- Keeping discussions reasonably relevant to the project or community space they are taking place in.
- Helping maintain a community where people feel comfortable contributing.

Technical disagreements are completely normal.

Personal attacks are not.

## What Is Not Acceptable

The following behavior is not welcome in Bourbon community spaces:

- Harassment, threats, intimidation, or bullying.
- Personal insults or targeted hostility.
- Trolling intended primarily to disrupt discussion.
- Discriminatory or derogatory comments.
- Sexual harassment or unwanted sexual attention.
- Repeated unwanted contact after someone has asked for it to stop.
- Deliberately provoking arguments across GitHub, Discord, or other platforms.
- Publishing another person's private information without permission.
- Sharing private conversations, addresses, email addresses, credentials, or identifying information maliciously.
- Encouraging harassment of another user, contributor, project, or community.
- Abusing bug reports, issues, pull requests, or support channels to target individuals.
- Knowingly providing malicious code, malware, credential stealers, or harmful downloads while presenting them as legitimate Bourbon resources.
- Any other behavior that Bourbon moderators reasonably determine is harmful to the community.

## Technical Criticism

Bourbon is a software project, and criticism of the software is welcome.

You are allowed to say:

- A feature is broken.
- A design is confusing.
- A compatibility change caused a regression.
- A proposed implementation is unsafe.
- A pull request should be changed.
- Bourbon made the wrong technical decision.

Criticism should focus on the **code, behavior, design, or decision**, not the person behind it.

For example:

> "This implementation blocks the main thread and should be moved off MainActor."

is useful technical feedback.

Attacking the contributor who wrote it is not.

## Bug Reports and Diagnostic Information

Bourbon frequently deals with Wine crashes, Windows installers, process information, filesystem paths, and diagnostic logs.

Please be careful when sharing diagnostic information publicly.

Before posting logs or screenshots, check for:

- Usernames
- Home-directory paths
- Email addresses
- Authentication tokens
- API keys
- License information
- Private server addresses
- Personal files or folder names
- Other identifying information

If a maintainer asks for diagnostic information that may contain private data, use a private method of sharing it when one is available.

Do not repost another user's private diagnostics without permission.

## GitHub

This Code of Conduct applies to Bourbon's GitHub activity, including:

- Issues
- Pull requests
- Discussions
- Code review
- Commit discussions
- Bug reports
- Feature requests
- Documentation contributions

Maintainers may edit, hide, lock, or remove content that violates these standards.

Pull requests and issues may also be closed when they are primarily being used to harass, spam, or disrupt the project.

Closing a pull request because of technical concerns is not disciplinary action.

## Discord and Community Spaces

The same general expectations apply in the Bourbon Discord and other official Bourbon spaces.

Join the Bourbon Discord:

https://discord.gg/bpm6EGSVMR

Community conversations can be more casual than GitHub discussions, but harassment, threats, doxxing, targeted abuse, and repeated disruptive behavior are still not acceptable.

Moderators may remove messages, restrict access to channels, mute users, or remove users from the community when necessary.

## Reporting a Problem

If someone is violating this Code of Conduct, contact the Bourbon moderation team privately through the official Bourbon Discord.

Do **not** post sensitive incident details publicly if doing so could expose private information or escalate harassment.

Reports should include as much useful information as reasonably possible, such as:

- What happened
- Where it happened
- When it happened
- Relevant message or issue links
- Screenshots if appropriate
- Whether the behavior is ongoing

Reports will be reviewed as fairly as possible.

People handling reports should protect the privacy of reporters and affected individuals where practical.

## Enforcement

Bourbon maintainers and community moderators are responsible for enforcing these standards in official Bourbon spaces.

Moderation decisions should consider:

- Severity
- Intent
- Harm caused
- Whether the behavior is repeated
- Whether previous warnings were ignored
- Risk to other community members

Not every mistake requires a ban.

Likewise, serious behavior does not require repeated warnings before stronger action is taken.

## Enforcement Levels

### 1. Informal Correction

Used for minor or accidental behavior that can reasonably be corrected quickly.

Possible actions:

- Reminder of community expectations
- Request to edit or remove a comment
- Request to move a conversation to a more appropriate channel

### 2. Formal Warning

Used when behavior is clearly inappropriate or when earlier guidance has been ignored.

Possible actions:

- Written warning
- Removal of offending content
- Temporary restrictions on particular community spaces

Continued violations may result in stronger action.

### 3. Temporary Restriction or Ban

Used for serious incidents or repeated violations.

Possible actions:

- Temporary Discord mute or ban
- Temporary restriction from project discussions
- Temporary limits on community participation

The duration depends on the circumstances.

### 4. Permanent Ban

Used for severe misconduct or a sustained pattern of harmful behavior.

Examples may include:

- Serious harassment
- Threats
- Doxxing
- Malicious targeting of community members
- Repeated violations after previous enforcement
- Deliberately distributing harmful software through Bourbon community spaces

A permanent ban may apply across Bourbon's official community spaces.

## Maintainers and Moderators

Maintainers and moderators are also expected to follow this Code of Conduct.

A maintainer's position does not make harassment, retaliation, or abuse acceptable.

Moderation authority should be used to protect the project and its community, not to settle personal disagreements.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies in official Bourbon community spaces and when someone is clearly representing Bourbon in an official capacity.

It does not attempt to control unrelated private activity simply because someone happens to use or contribute to Bourbon.

However, conduct outside Bourbon's spaces may become relevant when it directly involves harassment, threats, doxxing, impersonation, or targeted abuse of Bourbon community members.
